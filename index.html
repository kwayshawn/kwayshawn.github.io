Calm Is a Skill: What Running Incidents Taught Me

Anyone who has worked in IT operations knows the feeling: the alert fires, the bridge fills up, and suddenly everyone wants an answer at once. Early in my career I thought the job was to have the answer. Over time I learned the job is to create the conditions where the answer gets found fast.

Three habits make the biggest difference for me:

1. Name one incident lead, out loud. Ten smart people talking over each other is slower than one person directing traffic. The lead doesn't have to be the most technical person on the call; they have to keep the room focused.

2. Separate fixing from updating. Engineers fix. Someone else owns stakeholder updates on a steady cadence, even when the update is "no change, still working." Silence creates more pings than bad news does.

3. Write it down while it's hot. A rough timeline captured during the incident beats a perfect one reconstructed a week later. That timeline becomes the backbone of a blameless review, and blameless reviews are where reliability actually improves.

None of this is flashy. But calm, repeated consistently, is contagious. And in operations, calm is a competitive advantage.
